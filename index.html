BUILD YOUR DEFENCES

Small checks. Big difference.

Use this checklist whenever a message asks you to act, click, pay or share information.

QUICK GUIDE
01@

Inspect the sender

Read the full email address or phone number. A familiar display name does not prove who sent the message.

Check: spelling, extra words, unexpected domains and unusual reply-to addresses.
02!

Slow down urgency

Scammers create pressure so you react before thinking. Unexpected threats, prizes and deadlines deserve extra scrutiny.

Check: “account suspended”, “act immediately” and “last warning”.
03↗

Verify the destination

Do not trust a link just because its text looks familiar. Navigate to the organisation's known website yourself.

Check: misspelled domains, shortened links and unexpected sign-in pages.
04⌁

Treat attachments carefully

Unexpected files can contain harmful content or lead to credential-stealing pages. Confirm with the sender first.

Check: unexpected invoices, delivery notices and unfamiliar file types.
05◇

Question the request

Legitimate organisations should not ask you to reveal passwords, one-time codes or sensitive details through an unsolicited message.

Check: requests for passwords, MFA codes, bank details or gift cards.
06✓

Verify independently

Use a phone number, app or website you already know. Do not use contact details supplied in the suspicious message.

Remember: report suspicious messages using your organisation's process.
⬡
The safest response is often to pause.

Do not reply, open attachments or follow links while you are unsure. Verify independently first.

ABOUT PHISHSWIPE

Make awareness stick.

Cybersecurity learning should be practical, memorable and easy to practise.

LEARN BY DOING
⬡

Phish or Legit?

PhishSwipe is a gamified phishing-awareness web application. Users review realistic email and SMS scenarios, swiping left to flag phishing and right to mark legitimate messages as safe. After each decision, the app explains the warning signs and the reasoning behind the correct answer.

It turns passive advice into short, repeatable practice. It is an educational tool—not a spam filter, threat-detection service or substitute for an organisation's security controls.

01

Realistic scenarios

Practise with examples inspired by common email and SMS social-engineering patterns.

02

Immediate feedback

Each answer is followed by clear explanations and highlighted clues.

03

Safe by design

All scenario domains are fictional placeholders. This demo does not send messages or open suspicious links.

04

Room to grow

The scenario dataset and game engine are structured to support future lessons, accounts and shared leaderboards.
